How to choose a textile sourcing agent in India

By Vaishali Khairnar, Founder, Global Synctex Solutions · 25+ years in Indian textiles

India has the mills to make almost any technical fabric your brand needs. What it often lacks is evidence you can check from abroad. Here are eight checks to tell a good textile sourcing agent in India from a poor one, before you send your first tech pack.

Ripstop shells, stretch trekking fabric, recycled polyester, coated and laminated cloth: Indian mills make all of them. But many capable mills in Surat, Ludhiana and Tiruppur have no website, no published spec sheets and no test reports in English. A good sourcing agent closes that gap. A poor one adds a margin and a layer of confusion.

1. Do they specialise in your product?

“We source everything” usually means “we know nothing deeply”. Ask which fabric constructions and end uses the agent handles most often. An agent who sources outdoor and performance fabrics should talk easily about denier, GSM, hydrostatic head, MVTR and DWR durability. If they can’t, they will rely on the mill to explain your spec, and that is where errors start.

2. Have they worked inside Indian mills?

The best agents have sat on both sides of the table: developing and selling fabric at a mill, and buying for international brands. That experience tells them which mills can really hit a spec and which only say they can. Ask about their career, not just the company’s age.

3. Will they show you real swatches before you commit?

Photos and catalogue PDFs are easy. Ask the agent to courier real swatches or a sample album with GSM, width, composition and finish written on each card. If they won’t, they probably don’t have a working relationship with the mill.

4. Do they visit and vet the mill?

Ask how they choose mills for your shortlist. A serious agent visits the unit, checks its machinery and capacity, and asks for its social-compliance audits (such as amfori BSCI, SMETA, WRAP or SA8000) before recommending it. They should name the mill and share its documents with the quotation. An agent who hides the mill’s identity until you pay is a red flag.

5. Do they speak your QA team’s language?

Performance claims mean nothing without a test method. Your agent should agree methods and pass levels with you before bulk, for example ISO 811 for waterproofness, ISO 4920 or AATCC 22 for spray rating, ISO 12947-2 for abrasion, and AATCC 183 for UPF. They should then collect lab reports from the mill or an independent lab.

6. Can they prove sustainability claims?

If you need recycled, PFAS-free or certified fabric, ask for the paperwork, not the adjective: GRS scope and transaction certificates for recycled content, OEKO-TEX certificates, and written confirmation that the DWR is fluorine-free (C0). An agent who shares certificates with every quotation will save your sustainability team weeks.

7. How do they control quality before shipment?

Ask who inspects bulk, when, and against what. The answer you want: inspection at the factory against your approved sample, at the AQL level you set, before goods leave. Ask to see a sample inspection report.

8. Is the fee model clear?

Agents charge in different ways: a commission on order value, a fixed service fee, or a mix. Neither is wrong, but you should know which one before you start, and whether the agent also takes money from the mill. See what a textile sourcing agent in India charges for typical structures.

Red flags

  • Won’t name the mill or let you speak to it.
  • Quotes a price before seeing your spec, GSM or finish.
  • No test reports, only “good quality” assurances.
  • Asks for payment to the agent’s own account for goods.
  • Claims to source every category in every country.

Questions to ask on the first call

  1. Which three fabrics like mine have you sourced in the last year, and from which regions of India?
  2. Can you send swatches with spec cards for two or three options?
  3. Which test methods would you agree for my product?
  4. Who inspects bulk, and against which AQL level?
  5. How do you charge, and do you receive anything from the mill?

FAQ

Is a sourcing agent the same as a buying house?

They overlap. A buying house usually handles finished garments for large retailers, often under a long-term contract. A sourcing agent can be more specialised and flexible, for example matching one technical fabric to the right mill.

Do I still need an agent if I've found a mill online?

It depends on how much you can check from abroad. An agent can verify the mill, manage samples and testing, and inspect bulk, which reduces risk on a first order.

Which regions of India make technical fabrics?

Synthetic and technical weaving is concentrated around Surat in Gujarat and Bhiwandi in Maharashtra. Tiruppur is known for knits and activewear, and Ludhiana for knits and fleece.
